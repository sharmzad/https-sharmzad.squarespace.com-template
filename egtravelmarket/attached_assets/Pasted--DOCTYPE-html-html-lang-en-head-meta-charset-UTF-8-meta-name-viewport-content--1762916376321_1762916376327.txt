    <h2>Melodies Older Than Time</h2>
    <p>
      Music in Egypt is as ancient as its pyramids. Archaeologists have uncovered harps, flutes, and percussion
      instruments from over 4,000 years ago — proof that rhythm and harmony have always been woven into Egyptian life.
      Even in temples, music was used to honor gods and heal souls.
    </p>

    <h2>The Golden Voices</h2>
    <p>
      The 20th century gave birth to Egypt’s musical icons — voices that transcended generations.
      <strong>Umm Kulthum</strong>, known as “The Star of the East,” filled concert halls with emotion;
      <strong>Abdel Halim Hafez</strong> sang of love and hope;
      and composers like <strong>Mohamed Abdel Wahab</strong> built the bridge between traditional
      and modern sound.
    </p>

    <p>
      Their songs are not nostalgia — they are living poems that still echo in taxis, cafés,
      and hearts across the Arab world. Each melody carries Egypt’s warmth, patience, and romantic soul.
    </p>

    <h2>Modern Harmony</h2>
    <p>
      Today, Egypt’s music thrives in all forms: classical orchestras at the Cairo Opera House,
      indie bands along the Nile, and Sufi gatherings where voices rise in devotion.
      This diversity reflects the Egyptian spirit — open, creative, and endlessly expressive.
    </p>

    <p>
      At EgTravelMarket, travelers can experience this living heritage firsthand —
      from elegant evenings of opera to intimate desert concerts under the stars.
    </p>
